# The Mechanic · FinWise

> Module 3 · Retention & Engagement. The engagement mechanic that builds the habit loop, with rationale and a wireframe.

## The mechanic

_The habit loop: trigger → action → reward → investment._
### Cash Safety Buffer

FinWise turns the user’s financial insights into a visible, ongoing goal: build and maintain a healthy cash runway.

Instead of rewarding users for simply opening the app, FinWise shows progress toward a meaningful business outcome — for example:

**Current cash runway: 5.2 weeks**  
**Target: 6 weeks**

Each week, FinWise identifies one or two actions that could improve the buffer, such as:

- Collect an overdue invoice
- Cancel an unused subscription
- Reduce an unusual recurring expense

When the user completes an action, FinWise shows the measurable effect:

**+2.0 days of runway**

The progress is saved and becomes the starting point for the next weekly review.

### Behavior to repeat

Return weekly, review a meaningful financial insight, and take one useful financial action.

### Natural cadence

**Weekly**

Small-business finances change frequently enough for a weekly check-in to be useful, without creating the noise or anxiety of a daily streak.

### Habit loop

**Trigger → Action → Reward → Investment**

**Trigger**  
FinWise sends a weekly prompt:

> “Your weekly cash check-in is ready.”

**Action**  
The user opens FinWise, reviews the latest financial change, and completes one recommended action.

**Reward**  
FinWise shows the measurable impact on the business:

> “This action added approximately 2 days to your cash runway.”

**Investment**  
The user’s progress, completed actions, and financial context are saved. That makes the next review more personalized and gives the user a reason to return.

Over time, the external reminder should become an internal habit:

**“I should check how my business is doing this week.”**

## Rationale

_Why this drives retention before the paywall._
FinWise’s Aha moment is not simply seeing a dashboard. It is learning something meaningful about the business from real financial data.

The Cash Safety Buffer extends that Aha moment into a recurring behavior.

Instead of using points or rewards disconnected from value, the mechanic rewards the user with something intrinsically useful: clearer financial control and measurable progress toward a safer cash position.

The course emphasizes that gamification should reinforce a behavior users already find meaningful, rather than adding hollow rewards. FinWise therefore does not reward logins; it rewards completing a real financial action.

The progress mechanic also avoids a punitive streak. Missing one week does not erase progress. The user simply returns to their current cash position and continues improving it.

Collaboration remains optional: after reviewing the plan, the user can share it with an accountant or bookkeeper, which can further embed FinWise into the business workflow.



## Churn defense

FinWise currently has high one-year churn, but the existing data does not prove whether this is primarily **price churn** or **value churn**.

The course distinguishes:

- **Value churn:** engagement declines before cancellation
- **Price churn:** engagement remains healthy until the billing or renewal decision

The Cash Safety Buffer is designed specifically to address **value-continuity risk** by repeatedly showing the user useful financial progress.

It should not be presented as a solution to price churn.

FinWise should combine cancellation feedback with behavioral data to determine which churn type is actually occurring.

---

## Why this mechanic

I chose a **progress-based mechanic** rather than a leaderboard or traditional streak.

A leaderboard would not fit the private and sensitive nature of small-business finances.

A strict streak could encourage users to open FinWise just to preserve a number, rather than because they have a meaningful financial decision to make.

The Cash Safety Buffer instead makes progress visible around a real business goal.
