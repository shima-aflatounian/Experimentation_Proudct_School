# The Model · FinWise

> Module 6 · Pricing & Monetization. The pricing and packaging recommendation for FinWise.

## Stage + model

### Stage 1 · Value Creation

FinWise has reached product-market fit and $10M ARR, but the growth economics still show that repeatable value is not yet strong enough:

- Trial-to-paid conversion remains around **2%**
- Only **40% of paid users are retained after one year**

Because both conversion and retention are weak, I would not treat pricing as the primary growth lever yet.

The immediate priority is to prove that users repeatedly experience enough financial value to justify paying and staying.

### Monetization model

**Subscription**, delivered through a **reverse-trial experience**.

Subscription fits FinWise because the product delivers value continuously through:

- ongoing financial monitoring
- personalized insights
- recurring financial reviews
- alerts about meaningful changes

The reverse trial gives users access to the full product before the paid boundary, which creates an opportunity to let users experience real value before asking them to pay.

---

## Recommendation

### Do not optimize the price point yet

There is not enough evidence to recommend either raising or lowering FinWise's price.

Instead, I would keep the current subscription / reverse-trial structure and first validate whether users experience repeatable value before reaching the paid boundary.

The monetization sequence I would test is:

**Connect data**
→ **Receive first meaningful insight**
→ **Return for a second meaningful financial review**
→ **Reach the paid boundary**

The paid boundary should sit where users feel that FinWise has become useful enough that losing continued access would matter.

### Before changing price, diagnose churn

FinWise should also distinguish **price churn** from **value churn**.

Add a cancellation survey asking users why they are leaving, then combine the response with their recent product behavior.

For example:

- If users remain highly engaged until renewal and then cancel while citing price, that strengthens a pricing or packaging hypothesis.
- If engagement falls before cancellation, that points more strongly to insufficient ongoing value.

---

## The pricing bet

**Packaging matched to value**

The question is not yet:

> “Should FinWise charge more or less?”

The more useful question is:

> **“Is FinWise asking users to pay at the point where they have experienced enough repeatable value to understand why the product is worth keeping?”**

Once FinWise proves the Aha → repeat-value loop, the next pricing experiment could test whether moving the free/paid boundary changes conversion.

---

## Model signal

I would look for three signals before concluding that the monetization strategy is working.

### 1. Conversion clusters around the value boundary

Users who complete the first meaningful insight and return for another financial review should convert at materially higher rates than users who do not.

### 2. Retention holds after conversion

Improved trial conversion should not come at the cost of worse retention.

The goal is not simply to create more paying users — it is to create customers who continue receiving enough value to stay.

### 3. Churn diagnosis becomes clearer

If cancellation-survey responses and behavioral data show that highly engaged users disproportionately leave at renewal because of price, then FinWise has stronger evidence to test price or packaging.

If users disengage before renewal, FinWise should continue prioritizing value creation rather than price changes.

---

## What success would justify next

If repeat-value users convert and retain materially better, FinWise can move toward **Stage 2 · Revenue Expansion** and begin testing structural monetization questions such as:

- where the free / paid boundary should sit
- which capabilities belong in paid tiers
- monthly vs. annual packaging
- whether collaboration or advanced financial monitoring should become upgrade triggers

Until then, pricing optimization would risk treating a value problem as a pricing problem.
